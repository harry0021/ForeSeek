Motivation

From Reactive Retrieval to Predictive Exploration

Instead of probing the timeline blindly for locally salient clips, ForeSeek anticipates what unseen intervals should contain and turns failed expectations into signals for global backtracking.

Figure 1. Prior Agents vs. ForeSeek. Prior agents (top) reactively probe the timeline without explicit expectations about unseen content, whereas ForeSeek (bottom) maintains visually grounded predictions that continuously guide evidence collection.

Previous Video Agents

Reactive Retrieval Bottlenecks

  • Directionless exploration: Frame selection follows local saliency rather than a global temporal plan.
  • Silent negative evidence: Failed retrievals are discarded instead of constraining where to look next.
  • Irreversible commitment: Once a wrong temporal hypothesis is chased, beliefs are rarely revised globally.
ForeSeek Paradigm

Foresight-Driven Evidence Seeking

  • Falsifiable expectations: Predicts concrete visual cues expected inside an unseen interval before opening it.
  • Verification-driven updates: Confirms, narrows, or rejects hypotheses against targeted frame observations.
  • Principled backtracking: Failed predictions demote implausible intervals and redirect search globally.
Abstract

Summary

Long-form video understanding, as a fundamental yet crucial vision task, is being revolutionized by multimodal large language model (MLLM)-based video agents. However, the retrieval-driven nature inherent to these agents creates a significant bottleneck, forcing them to reactively fetch clips based on local saliency without a global temporal plan.

To address this, we present ForeSeek, a foresight-driven video agent that transforms long-video reasoning from reactive retrieval into predictive exploration. Rather than blindly probing the timeline, ForeSeek forms visually grounded expectations over unseen temporal regions, verifies them through targeted observations, and continuously refines its search based on emerging evidence, where failed expectations serve as informative signals for principled backtracking.

Furthermore, we develop a two-stage training framework (SFT on structured foresight trajectories followed by efficiency-aware GSPO) that equips MLLMs with foresight-guided reasoning and efficient decision making. Extensive experiments on five long-video benchmarks demonstrate that ForeSeek consistently improves the efficiency–accuracy trade-off of long-video reasoning, achieving competitive or superior accuracy while observing 2.5–4.4× fewer frames than the strongest search-based agent.

Method

Predictive State, Verification & Two-Stage Training

At step r, ForeSeek maintains a predictive state Sr = (Br, φr, H<r): a global interval belief map, a visually grounded expectation for the next interval, and the verification trace.

Click to expand

Figure 2. Overview of ForeSeek. Phase 1 performs a duration-adaptive sparse scan to initialize the temporal belief map and initial prediction. Phase 2 samples targeted frames inside the chosen interval, verifies the stated expectation, updates interval relevance labels, and either answers, narrows, or redirects globally.

1 Phase 1 · Sparse Initialization

Duration-Adaptive Global Scan

A query-aware scan covers the full video with a logarithmic frame budget, giving rapid global coverage on short clips without oversampling hour-long videos.

N(T) = clip(⌊10 + 3·log2(1 + T/600)⌉, 10, 20)

From these 10–20 frames and question q, the policy produces the initial global interval belief B1 and first expectation φ1.

2 Phase 2 · Predictive Verification

Check Before Choosing Next

Each round inspects 4–16 frames inside the target interval and verifies whether observations support φr, returning one of three structured verdicts:

Confirmed Evidence validates φr; the agent outputs the final answer.
Partially Confirmed Scene matches but fine detail is missing; narrows to a tighter sub-interval.
Not Found Prediction is rejected; interval is demoted and search redirects globally.
3 Belief & Two-Stage Training

Global Belief + SFT → GSPO

Every interval tracks a textual hint and an ordered relevance state guiding global scheduling:

HIGH MEDIUM LOW ELIM

Stage 1 (SFT): 8,165 foresight trajectories teach explicit expectation, verification, and backtracking.

Stage 2 (GSPO): 4,663 pass@8-filtered prompts optimize multiplicative efficiency reward:

R(τ) = Rans(τ) · T(n),  T(n) = 1.0 (n<4) → 0.8 (n=5)

LVBench · #1210 · Va_9Q6ekm60 · 35m 30s

What is in the pocket of the shop assistant’s clothes?

A. A lollipop B. A pencil C. A protein bar D. A signature pen ✓
R1 · 1775–1893s Not Found

Expected the apron pocket. Observed frames only show hands lifting fish-shaped waffles from a machine.

R2 · 1656–1775s Partially Confirmed

Redirected earlier. White gloves and a blue apron appear, but the pocket item is still unreadable.

R3 · 1740–1760s Confirmed

Zoomed to a 20s window. A black pen with a silver clip sits inside the apron pocket. Answer D.

Which country does the second athlete represent when he takes out a flag?

A. Poland B. Kenya ✓ C. Algeria D. Norway

Expected a flag celebration near the finish. Frames only show runners crossing the line and a leaderboard.
